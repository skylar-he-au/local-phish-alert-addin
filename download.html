Local Phish Alert

An Outlook add-in that checks each email you open for phishing and scams, including fake payment requests from "colleagues". The check runs on your own Mac: email never leaves it.

Download for Mac

LocalPhishAlert.dmg · about 1 MB · the installer then downloads Ollama and the AI model (about 2 GB).
Needs macOS 14 or later, about 3 GB free, Chrome, and Outlook on the web.

Install

  1. Open LocalPhishAlert.dmg from your Downloads folder.
  2. Double-click Install Local Phish Alert.
  3. Follow the steps. The installer sets up everything on the Mac by itself: Ollama (the free engine that runs the AI, in the background with no window), its setting, and the model. At the end it puts the add-in file in your Downloads folder, opens Outlook's add-in page and tells you where to click: Add from File, then that file.

If you already use the Ollama app, the installer uses it instead: then keep Ollama running (llama icon in the menu bar) and do not choose Quit Ollama.

If macOS says the app "cannot be opened" or is from an unidentified developer: choose Done, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, choose Open Anyway next to the app's name, and confirm. This is needed once per app, because this student project is not signed with a paid Apple developer certificate.

Use

  • Work or school account (Microsoft 365): open any email → Apps → Local Phish Alert → Open panel, and pin the panel. From then on every email you open is checked, and the result appears above the email.
  • Outlook.com: open an email → Apps → Local Phish Alert → Quick check.
  • The first time, Chrome asks whether the site may access apps on this computer. Choose Allow.

The AI's answer is an opinion. A warning means "check before you act"; no warning does not prove an email is safe. When an email asks for a payment, bank details or a new phone number, check with the sender another way.

Uninstall

  1. Open LocalPhishAlert.dmg again (or download it again).
  2. Double-click Uninstall Local Phish Alert and follow the steps. It removes the setting, deletes the model unless you keep it for a later reinstall, removes the Ollama server it installed (or, if you like, your Ollama app), keeps the model if you like, and opens Outlook's add-in page to remove the add-in there.

What it installs

PartWhere
Ollama, the engine that runs the AIIts background server, in Library › Application Support (skipped if you already have the Ollama app)
The model llama3.2:3b, about 2 GBOllama's folder
One setting that lets the add-in, and nothing else new, use OllamaA login item in ~/Library/LaunchAgents
The add-inYour Outlook; its files are served from this site
Senders you choose to trust, if anyYour own mailbox (add-in settings)

Email content is sent only to Ollama on this Mac (localhost). Nothing is collected, and this site hosts no email.

For IT departments

Deploy the add-in to users from the Microsoft 365 admin center → Settings → Integrated apps, with manifest.xml. Users then skip step 4 of the installer. The Chrome policy LocalNetworkAccessAllowedForUrls with https://skylar-he-au.github.io removes Chrome's question, and the model can be pre-loaded with ollama pull llama3.2:3b.
